# AxTrade | Winter Analyst Training Program 

## Finance Distribution and Brokering

This repository documents my four-week internship programme at AxTrade Capital Ltd, focused on finance distribution, brokering, structured products, asset management certificates (AMCs), fundraising, and private debt.

The programme developed my understanding of market research, product positioning, client engagement, investor communication, transaction structuring, and risk-aware financial analysis. The work culminated in a final presentation that synthesised weekly learning and proposed growth initiatives for the business.

> **Important:** This repository contains educational and internship work. Market observations, client-outreach plans, product proposals, target metrics, and transaction structures were prepared for learning and strategic-discussion purposes. They do not represent executed transactions, live investment performance, or investment advice.

## Programme structure

### Week 1 — Structured products

I researched structured-product market developments and considered how products can be positioned for professional and high-net-worth clients in volatile markets.

The work covered:

- Equity-linked notes (ELNs), including equity-return potential and principal-risk considerations.
- Dual-currency deposits, including foreign-exchange yield opportunities and currency-depreciation risks.
- Autocallables and inflation-linked notes for private-bank and institutional client segments.
- The role of capital-protection features when market uncertainty is elevated.
- Asia FX-linked structured products as potential hedging tools in a changing geopolitical environment.

A core lesson from this work was that product recommendations must clearly communicate downside risks, suitability considerations, and relevant regulatory requirements. A product’s potential return is not meaningful without an appropriate explanation of its risks and the client’s objective.

### Week 2 — Sales strategy and client engagement

I developed a phased sales and client-engagement framework for Asian market segments. The framework linked market research, client profiling, personalised outreach, follow-up activity, and performance measurement.

The strategy included:

- Identifying target client segments and their likely product needs.
- Defining an AI-pricing value proposition focused on potential execution-cost savings.
- Using digital channels, events, and partnerships to build a distribution pipeline.
- Designing personalised outreach that links potential benefits with product risks.
- Using CRM-style tracking and feedback to manage engagement and improve conversion.
- Setting illustrative KPIs for conversion, AUM growth, client satisfaction, and sales-cycle efficiency.

The main takeaway was that effective follow-up must add value. Each client interaction should offer a new market insight, relevant risk analysis, or useful perspective instead of simply requesting another meeting.

### Week 3 — AMCs, tokenisation, and fundraising

I studied actively managed certificates (AMCs), their use of ISINs, and their position between traditional financial products and emerging tokenised investment structures.

My work included:

- Reviewing AMC product concepts and market positioning.
- Comparing examples of equity-growth, multi-asset, and ESG-themed AMC strategies.
- Assessing the potential role of asset-reference tokens (ARTs) in improving accessibility and liquidity.
- Preparing a fundraising concept for a hypothetical global-macro tokenised portfolio.
- Drafting a one-page investor mandate covering strategy, fees, asset allocation, risk factors, and benchmarks.
- Conducting three simulated pitch sessions and revising materials in response to feedback.

This work reinforced that investor materials must be tailored to the needs of professional investors. Compliance considerations, including MiCA-related requirements, should be addressed as part of the product and distribution strategy rather than treated as a separate final check.

### Week 4 — Private debt and brokering

I researched private-debt market trends, major participants, and potential opportunities in Hong Kong’s hospitality sector. The analysis considered how private credit and brokering may support refinancing where conventional bank financing is less available.

I developed an illustrative hotel-refinancing case with the following structure:

- A hypothetical USD 50 million refinancing requirement.
- A five-year mezzanine-financing structure.
- A 12% illustrative annual yield.
- A 2% illustrative arrangement fee.
- Risk-mitigation measures including property security, an interest-coverage covenant, an amortisation schedule, valuation monitoring, and defined refinance-or-sale exit options.

This was a simulated case used to practise analysing a transaction structure, identifying commercial terms, and connecting risks to specific mitigants. It was not a completed or live transaction.

I also considered relationship-building in brokering through industry events, LinkedIn outreach, and value-added market materials. The focus was on developing long-term trust with potential clients, referral sources, legal advisers, asset managers, and other market participants.

## Growth recommendations

Based on the research and programme work, I presented the following strategic recommendations for discussion:

1. Explore tokenised private-debt opportunities connected to Hong Kong hospitality refinancing.
2. Consider hybrid offerings that combine structured-product features with AMC strategies.
3. Expand Asia-focused brokering partnerships and relationship-development channels.
4. Use a structured KPI framework to monitor growth, client engagement, risk management, and ESG-related activity.
5. Build family-office distribution capability as part of an illustrative pathway toward larger AUM.

These were recommendations developed during the internship programme, not announced AxTrade business plans or guaranteed forecasts.
